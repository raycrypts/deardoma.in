# the funnel and commerce story

## the free tools are the top of the funnel

the hack finder and the pre-check cost cents to run and answer the
question every buyer actually has first: is this name reachable, and is
it worth asking. available names get a registrar affiliate link.
taken names get "want it anyway? have donna ask the owner for $4.49."
the free tier isn't a teaser, it's the honest half of the product:
most domains should not be pursued, and the service that says so for
free is the one you pay when the answer is yes.

## the pricing is the market position

$4.49 to deploy, $3.99 on completion, no commission, no subscription.
the fees are sized to the deal the brokers won't take (their minimums
start around $199; donna-spec.md §1, needs verification against live
broker pricing before publishing). a micro-deal only closes when
nobody in the chain is paid a percentage, so nobody in the chain is.
dear domain stays out of the money flow entirely: buyer pays seller
directly, which is what keeps a $30 transaction free of escrow
licensing and its costs (donna-spec.md §2, principle 3).

## the moat, stated as a market claim

the domain acquisition industry treats every owner as a professional
squatter: the domainer holding out for leverage, who needs a broker's
playbook, a valuation argument and weeks of patience. most unused
domains are not held by that person. they're held by someone with one
name and a mild case of forgetting. the industry's costs are sized to
the adversary; the actual market is mostly amateurs, so the industry
prices itself out of almost all of it.

dear domain's whole pipeline is the reverse of that assumption: find
out who is really on the other end before writing, score the
confidence, ask when unsure, offer found money instead of negotiating
against an imagined portfolio. the complexity brokers can't afford at
$30 is done by an agent for about a cent per pre-check, and that is
the moat: it's not a better letter, it's knowing that the letter
should be kind and short.

## who it is for

the wedge is the indie founder or side-project maker: the right name
is parked under a privacy wall, the budget is coffee money, and a
broker won't take the call. they arrive through the free tools,
because they're already searching for the name.

then, in the order their pain arrives:

- **the hack hunter.** the finder scores the split before the
  checkout: restrictions, trustee routes, renewal prices, platform
  flags, search treatment. when the perfect split is taken, the same
  results page offers the $4.49 ask.
- **the returner.** someone who let a name lapse and lost it. the
  expiry watch turns a yearly manual check into an alert.
- **the consolidator.** owns the main domain, wants the idle
  siblings, at prices where asking a broker would be absurd.

not for: trademark disputes, brand squatters, four-figure
negotiations, professional portfolio holders. the pre-check flags
possible trademark issues and priced-to-sell listings, and the
service hands those back rather than fanning them (donna-spec.md §9,
§13).

## why an ai agent, said plainly

the product's face is donna, an agent who is open about being an ai
in every message. that is not a cost cut we hide, it's the design:
outreach works when it is polite, brief, boring and easy to refuse,
and a bounded agent is those things by construction. the limits are
in code, not in a policy page: one first message and one follow-up
per channel, hard campaign caps, a permanent global suppression list,
and an honest 80-word letter with a one-word exit. a human
correspondence can always escalate; the state machine cannot.

## the honest limits

- the deploy fee pays for the attempt, not the result, and many
  campaigns will end in no response. the pre-check estimates the odds
  before you pay; believe it (donna-spec.md §14).
- no escrow. the buyer pays the seller directly. the recommended
  payment methods are the ones with buyer protection; anything else is
  the buyer's own risk.
- some registrars block transfers for 60 days after a registration or
  registrant change. donna detects it and tells both sides
  (donna-spec.md §10).
- no automated dms on social platforms, ever. no web3 outreach in v1.
- every message discloses that donna is an ai, which california
  requires of bots in sales conversations and which we'd do anyway
  (donna-spec.md §13; needs full read before publishing).
